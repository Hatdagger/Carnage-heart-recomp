# Negotiation in plain words (for the tutorials)

Written 2026-11-25 from the mapping in CAMPAIGN_RAM_MAP.md ("Negotiation and research"). Numbers are from Europa (Practice) and the game's code.

## What Negotiation is
Command menu > **Negotiation**. Six arms companies make the parts your OKEs are built from: Kawashima Corp, Brenan, Mcmorell, AAC, Union Claude and Canyon Logic. Each one makes its own range (bodies, engines, CPUs, weapons, ammo, armour, options). You start with a basic set of blueprints. To design better OKEs you buy more from these companies. Drakken, your enemy, does business with the same companies.

Pick a company, then one of four choices. Everything is paid for at once from your funds.

## Blue Prints: buy a design
- Shows what the company has on sale, by kind (OKE Body, Main Weapon, Sub Weapon, All Kinds). Pick one to see its figures and price, then OK.
- Once bought, the part can be used in Design and produced in the factory. You only buy a blueprint once.
- **The price changes**: up to 5% cheaper when you are losing the war, up to 15% dearer when you are winning. Companies give **regular customers** a better price (up to 15% off): buy from the same company often to become one. If you paid for a blueprint's development (R & D), you get 10% or 20% off it too.

## R & D: pay towards a new design
- Shows the blueprints the company is still developing. Pick one to see what it will be, then pay towards it (OK).
- Paying makes the design **arrive sooner** (the more you pay in total, the more turns are saved, down to a minimum) and **less likely to be delayed**.
- When it is finished you get a **discount** on buying it: 10% if you paid at least 80% of its price, 20% at 160%. Paying a lot also keeps Drakken from snapping it up for a few turns.
- Paying here also makes you a better customer of that company.

## Optimization: improve a body you own
- Only for some OKE bodies, and only once you own them. The screen shows what improves (for example lighter, tougher, cheaper to build) and how many turns it takes (12 for Prowler I).
- You pay once (16000 for Prowler I). When the turns are up, you get the **improved version** as a new blueprint, free.

## Information: ask for news
- Free. The company tells you what it hears: what a rival company is developing or has finished, or what it has sold to Drakken.
- Asking costs a little of the company's goodwill at the end of the turn, so don't overdo it.

## What happens each End Turn
- Companies move their developments on by one turn; when one is finished it goes on sale (sometimes it is delayed instead).
- Your optimizations count down.
- Every other turn companies forget a little: goodwill and "regular customer" status fade if you stop buying.
- Drakken buys blueprints, pays for development and orders optimizations with its own money. If you did not pay towards a new design, Drakken may buy it the moment it is finished.

## In an online game
- Your opponent takes Drakken's place: Information's "Drakken purchased our..." means your opponent bought it. The CPU does no business of its own.
- What you buy and pay for is yours; your opponent can buy the same blueprints. The companies' developments are the same in both games.
